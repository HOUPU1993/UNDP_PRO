Five chapters, one question

Where China gets hot, and why parts of Shenzhen feel it hardest

This atlas starts wide — nine summers of satellite land-surface temperature across roughly 300 Chinese cities — then narrows to a single city, Shenzhen, and asks whether its hottest ground is also its oldest. Every chart below is built from real satellite and building-footprint data; where a step hasn't finished running yet, that gap is shown honestly rather than filled in.

Chapter 01 · The wide view

National Heat Distribution (2018–2026)

Built from MODIS Terra/Aqua land-surface temperature (LST), averaged over each summer (June–September) across 475 Chinese cities and districts. Every number on this page — the coverage diagnostic, the national trend, and the full city ranking with its stability label — is real output from the source notebook's Step 5/7/8 pipeline, run to completion.

Summer MODIS valid coverage · nationwide

Coverage = the share of pixels nationwide with at least one cloud-free observation during that year's summer window (scale=5000m — a fast diagnostic, not the precise per-city statistic used elsewhere in this analysis). 2018–2021 and 2025 come out close to full coverage; other years read a little lower — that's real year-to-year cloud cover compounding with MODIS orbital drift (both Terra and Aqua have exhausted their station-keeping fuel and now cross the same spot at a slowly shifting time of day), not missing data or a rendering artifact. The actual spread is small — 97.1% to 98.6% — even where the underlying map looks visually very different between years.

City ranking & stability, 2026

Stability = a 9-point OLS trend (2018–2026) of each city's % hot area, labelled warming/cooling only at p<0.05 — see Chapter 04's method note for the same caveat about this kind of simplified significance test. City names are auto-romanized from the source Chinese administrative name (Hanyu Pinyin); a handful of uncommon-character names may romanize imperfectly, and 9 of the 475 units returned no valid LST pixels for 2026 (shown as —) — both are noted rather than patched over.
